# AuthorDesk user guide

## The app

AuthorDesk is a desktop app for Windows and Linux with its own built-in browser engine: you don't need Edge or Chrome. Your books live in its data folder (**File → Open data folder**), separate from the app itself, so updates never touch them. On Windows, **File → Add a desktop shortcut** or **Add to the Start menu**; with the Linux AppImage, **File → Add to the applications menu**. **View** has zoom (Ctrl+= / Ctrl+-) and full screen (F11).

Coming from an earlier version, which ran inside Edge or Chrome? Your books are copied over the first time the new app starts (the old copy is left alone). If the welcome screen shows no books, use **Your books from automatic backups → Restore** on that screen to bring each one back from its newest automatic backup.

## Getting around

- Press **Ctrl+K** (or click *Jump to…* at the top, or 🔍 on a phone) and type a few letters to jump to any chapter, character, place, note or board card, or run a command like *New chapter*, *Back up now* or *Theme: Night*.
- Press **F1** or **?** for help at any time.
- New here? Choose *Look around with a sample book* on the welcome screen.

## Themes

Click **◐** in the top bar to switch themes, or choose in Settings → Theme:

- **Everyday:** Paper, Sepia, Night, Sage (sprigs of sage behind the panels), Cozy Cottage (cream cards on cobblestones), and **Writing Desk** (a classic: an old honey-oak desk, writing paper and an oxblood-leather accent).
- **Genre themes:** **Romance** (deep wine and rose over faint lace), **Fantasy** (an enchanted forest: gold sparkles, crescent moons and little runes on emerald), **Mystery** (noir charcoal with faint question marks, magnifying glasses and fingerprints), **Spooky** (horror, but cute: bats, moons and pumpkins on deep purple), **Storybook** (children's books: pastel stars, hearts and crayon squiggles), **Wild West** (cacti, lucky horseshoes and sheriff's stars on sand) and **Starship** (sci-fi: cyan over faint circuitry and stars).
- *Match my device* uses Paper by day and Night when your device is dark.

**Customize** under any built-in theme opens it in the theme editor as your own version ("Romance (my version)"); the built-in one stays as it is. To start from scratch: Settings → Theme → *Create a theme*, pick five colours (background, panels, writing page, text, accent) and watch the whole app update as you go. AuthorDesk tells you if the text would be hard to read.

**Background picture:** in the same editor, *Choose a picture…* puts any photo or pattern behind everything (where the background colour shows; the writing page and panels stay plain). Choose **Fit to size** for one picture that fills the window, or **Repeat** to tile a pattern or texture. *Soften the picture* washes it with your background colour so the text on it stays readable. Large pictures are shrunk to a sensible size and kept with the theme. 

**Share a theme with a friend:** under one of your own themes, **Export** saves it as one `.authortheme.json` file with everything in it: the colours, the background picture (a built-in pattern too) and how it's fitted and softened. Your friend chooses **Import theme…** under the themes and picks the file; it's added as one of their themes and switched on (*Switch back* in the message undoes that). A theme with the same name as one they already have is added as "… (2)".

## Writing

- **Home** shows your book’s word count, today’s words (set a daily goal), recent scenes, quick-create buttons and backup status. **Continue writing** reopens where you left off.
- **Targets & length** (on Home): type a **book target** (80000, 80,000 or 80k) to see your progress and, from your last 14 days of writing, the date you’ll reach it. Give any chapter its own target in the table, or *Split the book target evenly*. The table shows each chapter's words and **estimated printed pages**, with a total for the book. Choose the size to estimate for: mass-market paperback, the KDP paperback and hardcover trims (5 × 8 to 6.14 × 9.21 in), or a standard manuscript page (250 words). The big number counts your chapters plus any Book pages you've actually written (a dedication, About the Author…), nothing more. For print sizes, the note under it also gives the finished paperback's length, with the title, copyright and contents pages and the blank pages that let chapters open on the right, like the paperback export. Below it, the **writing streak** calendar shows the last 12 weeks.
- **Pitch** (on Home): your book's **logline**, **blurb** and **synopsis**, saved with the book. With the AI Editor installed, *Draft with the AI Editor* writes a first version from each chapter's summary (or its scenes' summaries, its outline notes, or its opening lines). It's told to use only what your book says and to stop where your material stops, and it lists any name that appears nowhere in your book (an invented town, say) so you can check it. *Use as the eBook description* puts the blurb into the Kindle eBook's details. If chapters with text have no summary yet, the card offers to **summarize them** with the AI Editor first (it fills in only empty summaries; change them any time in Scene notes).
- **Write:** the chapter list is on the left (☰ or Ctrl+\\ to toggle), the page in the middle, tools on the right (◧). Each chapter has one or more scenes; with one scene the chapter simply *is* the scene.
- Everything saves automatically; the badge at the top shows *Saving soon… / Saving… / All changes saved*. If saving ever fails you’ll see a red banner — keep the window open.
- Toolbar: bold, italic, underline, heading, block quote, scene break (⁂), undo/redo, link to reference (🔗), find (🔍), snapshot (📸), focus mode (◎).
- **Move planning notes to Scene notes** (the same ⋯ menu): plan lines you typed into a chapter (“- Finn helps her clean up”) move out of the story text into each scene's Notes, so exports don't print them. The pre-publish checklist points out chapters that still have them. Each scene keeps a copy under Versions, and Undo puts everything back.
- **Tidy chapter titles** (the ⋯ menu beside the book's name above the chapter list): removes chapter numbers typed into titles (“Chapter 8.) An unwanted distraction” → “An unwanted distraction”), after showing you the changes; Undo puts them back. Chapters are numbered for you in exports: choose a heading style that shows the number.
- Chapter ⋯ menu: rename, move, add scene, insert chapter after, pin, put in a part, Move to Trash (restore it from 🗑 Trash at the bottom of the list). Double-click a chapter to rename it. Drag chapters or scenes to reorder; drop a scene on another chapter to move it.
- **Scene notes** tab (right panel): chapter/scene titles, summaries, status, POV, location, time marker, plot threads, notes, split at cursor, merge with next, create a board card. An empty time marker offers the time of the timeline event placed in that scene or chapter (*Use “day 1 7:32 P.M.”*).
- **Plan checklist:** lines in a scene's notes or a chapter's summary that start with a dash or bullet (“- Finn helps her clean up”) appear under them as a checklist to tick off while you draft. Points your text already seems to cover say *looks written* (the point-of-view character's name isn't needed, since a first-person scene doesn't use it), but only you tick them. Several points typed on one line (“- Winnie wears his number - Finn sees her - He's surprised”) can be split into a line each with *Split into 3 points*. The Board's Outline shows each chapter's and scene's progress (☑ Plan 2/5). While you write, the bar under the page shows the next point not ticked off yet (*Next: …*); click it to open the checklist.
- **Writing sprints:** *Sprint* on the writing toolbar starts a 15, 25 or 45-minute sprint. A small clock shows the time left and the words written so far (across every scene, and in Focus mode too); at the end you see the total, the words a minute, and how it compares with your best. *Stop* ends it early.
- **Find & replace in the whole book** (Ctrl+Shift+H, *Whole book…* in the find bar, Ctrl+K → *Find & replace in the book*, or **Rename everywhere…** on a character's entry): every match is listed with its sentence and what it will become; untick any you want to keep. With *Match case* off, capitals follow each match ("Jace" at the start of a sentence becomes "Finn" even if you type "finn"). If the word is a character's or place's name (or part of it, like "Jace" in "Jace Kensington"), it offers to rename the entry too. Every changed scene keeps a *before replace* version, and **Undo** in the message afterwards puts it all back.
- **Comments:** select some words and press **Ctrl+Alt+M** (or the speech-bubble button in the toolbar, or *Comment* in the menu that appears over a selection) to pin a note to them, like a comment in Word or Google Docs. Commented words are tinted and underlined; click them to read the comment, *Edit* it or *Resolve* it (remove it). The **Comments** tab in the side panel lists every comment in the scene; click one to jump to its words. Comments are only for you: they're saved with the scene but never appear in any export.
- **Highlights:** select words and click the highlighter (or press **Ctrl+Alt+H**) to mark them in the last colour you used; the ▾ beside it offers yellow, green, blue, pink, purple and orange, and *No highlight*. Clicking a highlighted selection again in the same colour takes it off. Like comments, highlights are left out of exports.
- **Versions tab:** snapshots of the scene, grouped by day, each with its time and how many words it gained or lost since the one before. Open one to see **what changed since** (removed words struck through, added words highlighted, unchanged paragraphs folded away, and how many words came and went) or the version as it was; copy it or restore it (your current text is snapshotted first).
- **Pacing strip:** the thin strip to the right of the page draws the scene's rhythm, one tick per sentence from top to bottom, wider for longer sentences. Dialogue ticks are in the accent colour and narration in grey, long sentences (30+ words) are orange, and an orange edge marks paragraphs of 150+ words. The outlined band is the part on screen; click anywhere on the strip to jump there. Hover it for a summary. Turn it off in Settings → Writing page; it hides in Focus mode.

## References

Create characters, places and more in **References**. While writing, select a name and press 🔗 to link it; click linked text to see the entry in the side panel. The panel also lists entries mentioned in the scene. Each entry shows where it is linked or mentioned. Archive entries to hide them without breaking links.

**In the story.** A character's entry shows a strip with a cell per chapter, darker where they're more present (mentions and lines of dialogue). It says where they're first and last seen, and warns when they're missing for five or more chapters (chapters you haven't written yet don't count). Click a cell to jump to that chapter. *Set first appearance* fills in that field in one click. Review → *Characters who drop out* lists everyone who disappears for a long stretch.

**Maps.** The **Maps** tab draws your World entries:
- **Relationships:** everyone with a connection, joined by lines labelled with the relationship ("sister", "rival"). Drag names to arrange them (it remembers); click one to open the entry; *Tidy up* lays it out again.
- **Family tree:** built from family words in characters' connections ("mother", "son", "sister", "wife", "grandmother", "aunt", "cousin"). A connection's label says what the *other* person is, as the entry shows it: on Winnie's entry, "Betty Hartley — grandmother" (the same way *Suggest relationships* writes them). One row per generation, oldest at the top.
- **Family names:** when a character's relatives call them "Grandma", "Grammy", "Mom" or "Dad" in the scenes those relatives narrate, the character's entry says so (*In Winnie Hartley's scenes, Betty is called “Grandma” and “Grammy”*) and offers *Add as nicknames*, so look-ups, mentions and the continuity checks find them under those names too. It stays quiet when the name could mean two people (two grandmothers).
- **Places:** give a place a picture (a sketch, a floor plan, a real map; it stays on this computer) and pin other places, characters or items on it. Click a pin for the scenes set there or mentioning it.

**Suggest relationships** (Maps → Relationships, with the AI Editor installed): *Read my notes* has the AI Editor read your characters' entries, your notes and your chapter summaries and suggest how characters are related (“Betty Hartley is Winnie Hartley's grandmother”), each with the words it comes from. It only keeps what the quoted words clearly support, and nothing is added until you click **Add** (both entries get the relationship).

**Series.** A project with more than one book is a series (add a book with *+ New book…* above the chapter list, or **Series → Bring in another project**). Its books share one set of characters, places and world notes, so every check, the AI Editor and continuity know they're the same people in every book. Bringing a project in merges entries with the same name (filling in empty details, never overwriting) and hides the old project without deleting it. The **Series** tab shows each entry's mentions per book and where they first appear. Continuity checks across books; for a death, write the book too: "deceased, Book 2 Ch. 14".

## Planning

- **Story Board:** lanes and cards; drag or use a card’s ⋯ menu to move. Open a card to set POV, location, threads, tags and notes, and to turn it into a scene or chapter. *Use a story structure…* adds lanes and beat cards for three-act, Save the Cat (15 beats), the Hero's Journey or the seven-point structure, each beat with what it does and where it usually falls. Then *Map my draft to the beats…* suggests a scene for each beat: where that beat usually falls by word count, or, with the AI Editor, the scene that does that job (best when your scenes have summaries). Change any, then *Link these*.
- **Outline** (Board → Outline): every chapter and scene with editable summaries.
- **AI summaries:** with the AI Editor, Scene notes → *Summarize with the AI Editor* drafts a 2–3 sentence summary of the scene (what happens and who) for you to use or discard, and *Summarize this chapter's scenes* does every scene without one, plus the chapter, for you to tick and keep. A summary says when the scene has changed a lot since it was written.
- **Timeline:** events with a date or relative marker and an optional order number. Possible continuity questions appear at the top — they are hints, not errors.
- **Plot Threads:** a grid of which chapters each thread appears in; long-quiet threads get a gentle reminder.

## Book pages

On your book's **Home** page, the *Book pages* card holds the pages around your story: a **Dedication** and an **Epigraph** (before the contents), and **Acknowledgements**, **About the Author** and **Also by** (after the last chapter). For an epigraph, put the attribution on its own line starting with a dash (— Who said it). For *Also by*, list one book per line. Write each one once and choose where it applies:

- **All my books (every project)**: handy for an author bio.
- **All books in this project**: for a series.
- **Only these books**: for a dedication to one book.

When you export (Word, plain text, Markdown or Amazon KDP), the right page for that book is picked automatically. You can choose a different one, or leave it out, in the export dialog.

## Proofreading

Suggestions appear as you write, a moment after you pause, like Grammarly or Google Docs. Underlines: wavy red = likely error, amber = worth checking, dotted blue = style idea (off by default; turn on in Settings). Click one to accept, ignore, add the word to your dictionary, or turn that check off. The **Suggestions** tab (its count shows beside it) lists everything and can apply a reviewed batch of fixes. Ctrl+Z undoes any accepted change. Checks you turned off are listed by name in Settings → Proofreading, each with a *Turn back on* button.

**Stronger words.** When a verb leans on an adverb or "very" props up an adjective, AuthorDesk offers one precise word in the same tense instead: *ran quickly* → *sprinted / raced / dashed*, *said angrily* → *snapped / growled*, *very tired* → *exhausted*. It finds the adverb a few words away too: *closed the door loudly* → *slammed the door*, *walked across the room slowly* → *ambled across the room*. Speech tags that say the same thing twice (*whispered quietly*, *replied back*) offer the plain verb.

**Dialogue assistant.** The side panel's **Dialogue** tab shows, for the open scene, how much of it is spoken, who speaks and how often (matching first names to your characters), which speech tags you use, and what might lose a reader: several lines in a row with no speaker named, two speakers in one paragraph, or a quotation that's never closed. A speech that runs on into the next paragraph, with its quote left open, is recognized as correct. Click any item to jump to it.

**Automatic backups.** While AuthorDesk runs, it saves a fresh copy of each book a couple of minutes after you stop writing (and at least hourly while you keep going) to `Documents\AuthorDesk Backups\<Book title>`. It keeps every copy from the last day, one a day for a month, then one a week, and always your newest five. **Backup → Automatic backups** shows the last backup, has *Back up now*, *Change folder…* (pick a OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox folder to keep a copy off this computer) and *Open folder*. Restore any of them with Settings → Restore a backup. **A second copy:** *Choose a second folder…* (a USB drive, or a OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox folder) and every backup is copied there too, kept the same way. If it isn't there when a backup runs (the drive is unplugged), the main backup still happens and the card says the copy was skipped.

**Review (the whole book at once).** The **Review** tab reads the entire manuscript and shows what a scene-by-scene check can't: reading ease, sentence length and variety, dialogue share, and adverbs, crutch words, passive voice, filler, speech tags and echo words per 1,000 words, chapter by chapter with a pacing bar (dialogue, fast, medium, slow). It measures the story only: planning lines left in a chapter (“- Finn helps her clean up”) aren't counted, and chapters with no story text yet are listed on one line. It lists your most-used words and repeated phrases, crutch words by chapter, "sticky" sentences (mostly little glue words), the longest sentences, slow stretches, and **continuity** problems. Click anything to jump to it. Nothing there changes your text. With the AI Editor installed, **Editor's notes** explains a chapter's main patterns (real errors like tense slips first, then style patterns that come up often) in plain words, each with an example sentence from the chapter.

**Character voices** (on Review) lists everyone who speaks: how many lines, their share of all the talking, words per line, their usual speech tags, and *their words*, the ones they use far more than anyone else. A line counts when a tag or action beat names the speaker (“Jace snapped”, “Finn shrugged. “…”); in first-person scenes, “I” counts for the point-of-view character set in Scene notes. Click a character's sample line to jump to it.

**Tense slips.** When a scene is mostly told in one tense, narration that drifts into the other ("I stepped inside… As I arrive on the floor, I make my way out") is underlined where each stretch starts, saying how many verbs it runs for. Dialogue, text messages and outline notes are left out, and a scene that's evenly mixed isn't flagged. The underline offers the verb in the scene's tense ("reach" → "reached"), and the AI Editor can put the whole sentence back in that tense. If a switch is on purpose (a flashback, a present-day frame), *Ignore* it; turn the check off in Settings if you mix tenses by design.

**Echo words.** The same noticeable word used again within about 50 words ("She glanced… He glanced back") gets a **teal dotted** underline. With the AI Editor installed, clicking it offers words that fit that exact sentence (same tense and sense), and opens the AI Editor with a "Without the echo" rewrite; otherwise you get general synonyms.

**Continuity.** When a character is described differently in different places (green eyes in Chapter 2, grey in Chapter 9), both places get a **purple** underline saying where the other is, with *Go to…* and *It changed on purpose*. If a character's entry says they died (Status: "deceased, Ch. 14"), lines they speak in later chapters are flagged too. With the AI Editor installed, Review → Continuity has **Mistake or on purpose? Ask the AI Editor** under each changed detail: it reads both passages and anything in between about that detail (a dye job, contacts, years passing) and gives its view in a sentence. You still decide.

**Ages and the timeline.** When a scene's age for a character ("Winnie, nineteen,") doesn't fit the timeline, it's underlined in purple too. It needs dates: link timeline events to scenes or chapters, with a *When* such as "March 2019", "summer 2021", or story time like "Day 12", "Week 3" or "Year 2" (story time and calendar dates are never compared with each other). Ages are checked against:
- the **Age** on the character's entry (a field named Age, or an "Age: 21" line), taken as their age at the start of the story;
- a birth date on the entry ("Born: 3 May 2002", "b. 1998");
- the age given in an earlier dated scene ("17 in March 2019, so 19 two years later").

A birth date or story time given only as a year allows a year either way. *This is fine* stops it being flagged again. All of it is listed under Review → Continuity.

**Hard-to-read sentences.** With the AI Editor installed, a long sentence built from many parts, or a very dense one, gets a thin **orange dashed** underline. Its pop-up offers the AI Editor's clearer version, with **Use**.

**Edit passes.** Review → *Start an edit pass* (the whole book or one chapter), or *Start one for this chapter* in an empty **Edit pass** tab, gathers every suggestion and review finding into a checklist, like an editor's notes. Work through it in the side panel's **Edit pass** tab: fixes in the open scene apply with one click (and Undo), others have *Go to*, and anything can be *Done*, *Dismissed* or saved for *Later…* with a note. Items find their place again after you edit; if you rewrite the words, the item counts as done. *Export as a report* saves the pass as a Markdown file to share with an editor.

**Using an AI suggestion.** Every AI rewrite (the AI Editor and the Dialogue tab's character voice) has a **Use** button: it puts that version in place of the passage it was written for, as one step you can undo (Ctrl+Z or the Undo in the message). If you've changed the passage since, it won't replace anything and tells you so.

**Paperback PDF.** Backup → Publish → *Paperback / hardcover* → File: **PDF, typeset by AuthorDesk**. You get a print-ready interior for KDP: exact trim size, mirrored margins with KDP's gutter for the final page count, chapters opening on right-hand pages a third of the way down, running heads (author / title), page numbers, a contents page with real page numbers, and no stranded first or last lines. Choose the trim size, text size, one of two included fonts (Crimson Text or Gentium Book Plus, both free for print) and a scene-break ornament; a preview appears before you upload. The Word option is still there if you'd rather adjust the layout in Word.

**Add-ons: set up once.** The first time AuthorDesk starts, it offers to install its two private add-ons, the grammar engine and the AI Editor (about 17 GB together; it checks your free space first). Both are ticked; untick either, or choose *Not now* (it asks again next launch) or *Don't ask again*. They download in the background while you write, with a small progress card in the corner, and each switches on the moment it's ready: no restart. If you close AuthorDesk mid-download, it carries on where it left off next time. **Settings → Add-ons** shows what's installed and has an *Install* button for anything that isn't. Installed add-ons **update themselves too**: when AuthorDesk starts and a new release pins a newer LanguageTool, model or llama.cpp build, it replaces just that part in the background (the add-on pauses for those minutes, then switches back on), checks free space first, and removes the old files. **Check** has each add-on do a small real job (the grammar engine checks a sentence with a known mistake; the AI Editor answers a simple question) and says plainly what's wrong if it fails. **Repair** checks its files and replaces only what's damaged, in the background. *Show the add-on log* is there for troubleshooting. You can still install them by hand with the scripts below.

**AI Editor (optional, on this computer).** Install it once from **Settings → Add-ons** (about 8 GB; it downloads in the background while you write). It uses llama.cpp and an open-source model (Mistral-Nemo 12B on graphics cards with 10 GB or more, Qwen2.5 7B otherwise): no account, no subscription, nothing sent anywhere. Open the side panel's **AI Editor** tab (or select text and choose *Ask AI Editor…*). It works on the selection or the paragraph at the cursor: **Rewrite**, **Smoother**, **Tighter**, **Without the adverb**, **Clearer**, **Fix grammar**, **Change tone**, **Show, don't tell**, **Character voice** (using that character's notes) and **Ask**. Results appear in the panel with the changes highlighted; nothing goes into your book until you click **Use** on a version. Clicking a flagged word shows the AI Editor's best version right in its pop-up too, with **Use**. Rewrites keep your tense (present stays present). **More → What's the tone?** names the passage's tone with the words that create it; put a line like `Tone: warm, hopeful` in the scene notes and it also says whether the scene gets there. Notes for any listed characters in the passage are used as background, so suggestions stay true to them. *Find character details in this scene* lists facts about your characters that their entries don't have yet, each with *Add to entry*. With the AI Editor installed, flagged adverbs are also read in context first: only the weak ones stay underlined, with the AI's reason in the hover text (turn this off in Settings → Proofreading → AI Editor). The model loads the first time you use it (10–20 seconds) and unloads after 15 idle minutes.

**Character details.** When you describe a listed character, AuthorDesk notices details their entry doesn't have yet, like *"Winnie pushed her auburn hair back"*, and underlines them in **green**; click to add "Hair: auburn" to her Physical appearance. A detail that contradicts the entry is underlined in **purple**: update the entry, or keep it and fix the text yourself. These only ever change the character's entry, never your book, and each has Undo. Underline colours: red wavy = likely error, purple double = contradicts a character or another chapter, amber = check, teal dotted = echo word, orange dashed = hard to read, blue dotted = style, green dashed = a character detail to add.

**Grammar engine on this computer (recommended).** For the most thorough checking (full-sentence grammar, verb forms, confused words like *affect/effect* from real usage statistics, and style), install the private grammar engine once from **Settings → Add-ons**. It downloads LanguageTool, its English usage data and a Java runtime (about 9 GB, into the AuthorDesk data folder; no admin rights needed). After that, AuthorDesk starts the engine by itself and uses it automatically: nothing leaves this computer and there are no rate limits. It takes a few seconds to warm up after AuthorDesk starts. Settings → Proofreading → *Deep grammar check* shows whether it's ready and lets you turn it off. Every underline explains itself when you hover over it; findings without a concrete fix are left out.

**Online grammar check (optional).** Settings → Proofreading → *Turn on online check…* adds deeper grammar and style checks from LanguageTool, a free service with no account. Before anything is sent, AuthorDesk explains exactly what goes where: only the paragraphs of the scene you're editing, a couple of seconds after you pause, and only paragraphs that changed. Suggestions from it are labelled *LanguageTool* in the Suggestions tab. It pauses while you're offline, and you can turn it off at any time. To keep text on machines you control, tick *Use my own LanguageTool server* and enter its address (for example `http://localhost:8081/v2/check` for LanguageTool running in Docker on this PC).

## Publishing on Amazon KDP

**Backup** → **Publish on Amazon KDP…** (or Ctrl+K → *Publish*).

1. Choose **Kindle eBook** (EPUB, recommended), **Kindle eBook (Word)**, or **Paperback / hardcover** (pick the trim size you'll select on KDP, a font and text size).
2. Choose how chapter headings look and which extra pages to include (title, copyright, **table of contents**). The book-page menus (Dedication, About the Author, and Epigraph, Acknowledgements and Also by once you've written one) pick the pages you wrote on Home (automatically, a different one, or none). eBook contents link to each chapter (Kindle's own Go To menu is always included). For print you can list chapters only, or add page numbers — those are filled in by Microsoft Word, so open the file in Word, choose **Yes** when it asks to update fields, and save before uploading. Curly quotes and em dashes are applied to the exported copy only — your manuscript isn't changed.
   For the Kindle eBook, pick a **style**: *Classic* (centred headings, the opening line in small caps), *Modern* (bold left-aligned headings, a large first letter) or *Minimal* (quiet headings, no ornaments), and the **scene break** (the same choices as the paperback). Kindle readers still choose their own font and size.
3. Fill in the book details (author name, copyright year, optional imprint and ISBN). They're saved with the book for next time.
4. Read the checklist at the bottom (it warns about empty chapters, leftover "TK"/"TODO" notes, and books under 24 or over 828 print pages), then save the file.
5. On kdp.amazon.com, upload it as the **manuscript** on the Content page. For print, choose the same trim size and **No bleed**, then check every spread in KDP's **Print Previewer**. For eBooks, check it in the **Kindle Previewer**. Covers are uploaded separately.

If you change the book later, export again and re-upload; KDP replaces the old file.

## Bringing in an existing book

**From Word or Google Docs:** choose *Start from a Word or Google Docs file* on the welcome screen or on *Your books*, or Backup → Import → *Choose a Word file (.docx)…* to add chapters to the book you're in. For a Google Doc, download it first with *File → Download → Microsoft Word (.docx)*. You'll see the chapters AuthorDesk found (from the document's headings, or lines like "Chapter 3", or page breaks) with their word counts, and can untick any; text before the first chapter (a title page) starts unticked. Bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, scene breaks, Word's comments and Word's highlights come across. Fonts, sizes and spacing don't: your export settings decide how the book looks.

**A whole project:** Backup → **Import your own files** (or *Bring in my existing book* on the welcome screen). Chapters go in a `chapters/` folder as `.md` or `.txt` files; characters, places, the story board, timeline and plot threads go in CSV spreadsheets. Every detail is in [IMPORTING.md](../IMPORTING.md).

## Backups

**Backup** → **Save backup** regularly and keep copies elsewhere (USB drive, cloud folder). To restore: **Restore an AuthorDesk backup** → choose the file → review → Restore, Merge, or Import as a copy. Exports to Word, text, Markdown, JSON and CSV are there too, plus **Outline (.docx)**: your plan to read or print, with each chapter's summary and plan, then each scene's point of view, place, time, word count, summary and notes, plan points marked ☐ or ☑ (and any plan lines still typed in the text).

## Keyboard shortcuts

| Keys | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Ctrl+K / Ctrl+Shift+F | Search everything |
| Ctrl+F | Find & replace in scene |
| Ctrl+Shift+H | Find & replace in the whole book |
| Ctrl+S | Save now |
| Alt+PageDown / PageUp | Next / previous chapter |
| Alt+Shift+PageDown / PageUp | Next / previous scene |
| Ctrl+Alt+F | Focus mode (Esc exits) |
| Ctrl+Alt+M | Comment on the selected words |
| Ctrl+Alt+H | Highlight the selection |
| Ctrl+\\ | Toggle chapter list |
